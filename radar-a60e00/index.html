Why 74% of Companies Expect Employee Costs to Rise

“74% of Indian companies expect higher employee costs because of new labor code rules.”

A Grant Thornton survey in Economic Times shows 74% of Indian organizations expect employment costs to rise under the upcoming labour codes. The new rule mandates that basic salary must make up at least 50% of total compensation. That spikes mandatory company contributions to provident fund and gratuity. Nearly half of surveyed firms expect costs to climb by up to 5%, forcing them to restructure salary slips.

India Millions of corporate employees across India could see their monthly in-hand take-home salary shrink even if their annual CTC stays the same.

At home Your monthly paycheck may feel smaller next year because the government is forcing your boss to save more of your money for retirement.

Joke HR spent two decades inventing fifteen weird allowances to save taxes, and the government wiped them all out with one fifty-percent rule.

Risk Viewers might think their company is cutting their total pay unless you clearly explain the difference between CTC and monthly take-home.

Why RBI Just Brought 60 Tonnes of Gold Back

“60 tonnes of gold just quietly landed in India from London, and the RBI is storing it here.”

The Reserve Bank of India brought back 60.08 tonnes of gold between March and September 2026. This raised India's domestically stored gold to 84%, or 740.13 tonnes out of its total 880.52 tonnes reserve. The official story is routine asset management. The real story is that after Western nations froze Russia's $300 billion foreign reserves, no central bank wants its physical gold sitting in British vaults.

India India now holds 84% of its sovereign gold on home soil rather than leaving it in London or Switzerland.

At home If your family prefers holding real physical gold in the house locker over bank paper, the RBI governor is running the exact same playbook.

Joke We spent decades politely asking the British for the Kohinoor, but for the gold bars, RBI just booked cargo flights and took them back.

Risk Viewers might confuse this repatriation with the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis when India had to pledge gold abroad.

Why the US Wants a 25% Tariff on India

“25% tariffs on Indian goods were just proposed by the US government in a new probe.”

The US Trade Representative proposed import tariffs of up to 25% on Indian goods following a Section 301 investigation. But Washington offered to lower that tax to 17% if India signs a bilateral trade deal. The official story is checking global manufacturing capacity and fair trade. The real story is that the US is using high tariff threats as leverage to force open India's domestic markets.

India A 25% tariff hits Indian textile, engineering, and pharma exporters that sell tens of billions of dollars worth of goods to the US every year.

At home When foreign trade taxes go up, export businesses earn less foreign exchange, putting direct downward pressure on the rupee in your pocket.

Joke The US offering to drop tariffs from 25% to 17% if we sign a deal sounds exactly like negotiating at a local flea market.

Risk Trade investigations involve diplomatic posturing that can be resolved or postponed before tariffs actually take effect.
